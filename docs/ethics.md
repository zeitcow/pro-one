# Ethics

Pro-One should expand access to legal information while respecting user autonomy, safety, privacy, and the limits of AI.

The project should not make legal help feel more certain than it is. It should help users understand options, sources, risks, and limitations without pressuring them into decisions.

## Core ethical standard

Pro-One should help users understand and organize legal information.

It should not pretend to be a lawyer, replace legal judgment, or hide uncertainty for the sake of product polish.

## User autonomy

Users should remain the decision-makers.

Pro-One should:

- explain options without choosing for the user
- show the sources used
- make assumptions visible
- let users review and edit drafts
- explain uncertainty clearly
- avoid pressuring users into legal decisions
- encourage users to verify information before acting

## Transparency

Pro-One should be transparent about:

- what it can do
- what it cannot do
- what sources were used
- what jurisdiction is being discussed
- what information is missing
- whether an answer is limited
- whether the system is uncertain

The system should not hide limitations to appear more capable.

## Fair access

Pro-One should be built around legal access.

The project should aim for:

- free core workflows
- plain-language explanations
- low-friction access
- accessible design
- printable or downloadable outputs
- future multilingual support only when quality can be tested
- no unnecessary paywalls around basic legal information workflows

Free access should remain central to the project.

## Avoiding false confidence

Legal AI can sound confident even when it is wrong.

Pro-One should reduce false confidence by:

- citing sources
- showing limitations
- refusing unsupported answers
- identifying jurisdiction
- warning users about deadlines
- distinguishing general information from case-specific judgment
- avoiding legal conclusions that require a lawyer

## No manipulation

Pro-One should not use dark patterns or pressure tactics.

The project should avoid:

- pushing users toward paid services
- making users feel they must rely on the system
- hiding safer alternatives
- overstating the system's reliability
- using fear-based messaging
- making legal choices for users

## Equity and accessibility

Legal access problems often affect people with fewer resources.

Pro-One should consider users who may have:

- limited money
- limited legal knowledge
- limited time
- limited English proficiency
- disabilities
- low digital literacy
- urgent legal stress
- limited access to lawyers

Design decisions should favor clarity, usability, and safety for these users.

## Responsible AI use

AI should be used to support legal access, not to create unsupported legal answers.

Pro-One should avoid:

- invented legal authority
- fake citations
- unsupported legal conclusions
- hidden reliance on weak sources
- unsafe automation of legal decisions
- treating model output as truth without source support

AI output should be reviewable, explainable, and connected to sources.

## Human help

Pro-One should not frame human legal help as unnecessary.

The system should help users understand when they may need:

- legal aid
- a court self-help center
- a court clerk for procedural questions
- a qualified attorney
- emergency assistance
- a trusted public-interest resource

## Small-business ethics

Small businesses may use Pro-One for disputes, contracts, invoices, customer issues, vendor issues, or employment-related matters.

The project should help small businesses organize and understand legal information without encouraging aggressive, misleading, or abusive legal tactics.

## Guiding rule

Pro-One should be useful, honest, and limited.

The project should expand access to legal information while making clear when AI is not enough.
